Insights

Perspectives on markets, strategy and AI.

Short, practical perspectives from USR's research and advisory work — market intelligence, strategy thinking, business intelligence and AI adoption.

Market intelligence

Why market sizing needs a defined decision, not just a number

A market-size estimate is only useful when it is scoped to answer a specific business question — entry, investment or resourcing. Sizing without a defined decision behind it tends to produce numbers that look precise but don't actually help anyone act.

Strategy

Evaluating market entry: the questions that matter first

Before a market-entry study begins, it's worth being explicit about three things: how attractive the market genuinely is, how intense the competitive response will be, and whether your go-to-market model can realistically work there. Sequencing these questions changes what research is actually worth commissioning.

Competitive intelligence

Benchmarking is only useful when it changes a decision

A competitor benchmarking exercise that simply lists features or pricing rarely moves a business forward. The more useful version connects each data point back to an implication: where you are exposed, and where you have room to act.

AI & technology

Where AI genuinely helps a small business — and where it doesn't

The highest-value AI use cases for smaller organizations tend to be unglamorous: faster first-response to customers, structured knowledge retrieval, and repetitive research or data tasks. Broader "AI transformation" ambitions are usually better deferred until these fundamentals are working.

Business intelligence

What separates a data dashboard from a decision tool

Most dashboards report what happened. A decision-support view goes one step further, tying each metric to a specific action a manager is expected to take when it moves.

Strategy

Business diagnostics: finding the real constraint

Growth problems are often diagnosed as a marketing or sales issue when the actual constraint sits elsewhere — in pricing, delivery capacity or product-market fit. A short diagnostic phase before committing to a strategy avoids solving the wrong problem well.
